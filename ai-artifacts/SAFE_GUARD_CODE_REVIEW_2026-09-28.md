## The judge's report

# Judge's ruling: `\<condition>[\<safe>] P` unified onto the out-of-run instance (phi-system, baseline 0110a6fa)

## Verdict: MET_ON_CONDITION

The code change is sound and has the right shape. Before, `\<condition>[\<safe>] ?P` and `\<condition>[\<no>-\<inst>-\<safe>] ?P` were registered to `Phi_Reasoners.wrap (safe_obligation_solver {can_inst}) o snd`, an unbounded clarsimp in `Phi_Safe_Simps`. Now they are registered to `Phi_Reasoners.guard_condition_solver {can_inst} o snd`, which is the factory (`gen_guard_condition_solver`) over `p_auto_solving`: guard_simp_tac, then P-auto once, with no digest, no store, no refuter and no judge, and the incoming context returned. The primed tactic forms are built on it, and the three `safe_obligation_solver*` functions are gone. I verified this in reasoners.ML:1680-1749 and PLPR.thy:2021-2034.

I found no wrong verdict, no crash, no dirty hack and no design defect in the code. What survives is in:
- the verification record: F2, a hard condition, and F1, the author's choice of scope;
- user-visible text: F4, F5 and F6, the author's wording;
- the tests, internal comments and plan: F3, F7, F8 and F9, which the drafter fixes and which then go back to review.

## Surviving findings

### F2: the "build lesson" is wrong, and nothing proves the Phi_Examples leg compiled the final bytes (major until checked). Ruling: drafter decides; hard condition.

**Claim.** Plan section 40 says an edit of reasoners.ML does not invalidate the consumer heaps "Phi_System_Base … PhiStd, which embed the reasoner". From this it derives the rule that a leg must move those heaps aside.

**Defense.** The defense concedes the lemma and the gaps in the record. It argues that the risk that the leg is void is narrower than claimed: a tree mixing old and new files cannot compile, because the new PLPR.thy names `guard_condition_solver` and the old one names `safe_obligation_solver`.

**What I verified.**
- Phi_System_Base/ROOT loads only foreign theories.
- Phi_Semantics_Framework imports Phi_BI as a session.
- The local `Phi_Semantics_Framework.db` lists both `…/PLPR.thy` and `…/library/reasoners.ML` in `isabelle_sources`.
- The working-tree SHA1s are 9ae22157… (reasoners.ML) and f2e2e224… (PLPR.thy).

So Isabelle does invalidate the chain from Phi_Semantics_Framework upward. The observed "nothing to do" has no identified cause, and the likeliest explanation is that the build read stale bytes. That would void the leg whether or not the heaps were moved aside.

**Ruling.**
- The read-only digest query on cslh19 must print the two SHA1s before the change is accepted. If it does not, the leg is rerun within the author's existing cslh19 build approval.
- The lemma is rewritten by hand, which also agrees with the author's standing position that Isabelle invalidates heaps by itself.
- The record must say how the gate was closed and where the five moved-aside heaps now are.

### F1: the safe guards changed simpset without a record, and a clean build cannot show a silent loss (major as a gap in verification). Ruling: must discuss with the author. The drafter writes the documentation part regardless.

**Claim.** Both the front rewrite and the prover moved from `Phi_Safe_Simps.equip ctxt0` to the run's simpset (plus `\<phi>guard_simp` for the prover). `\<phi>guard_simp` has no declarations anywhere. So the `\<phi>safe_simp` rules that are not [simp] no longer reach these guards; among them are prod.map_unspec and map_unspec_eq, whose equation shape matches Phi_Type.thy:4227. A failed safe guard only skips its rule, so a clean build cannot show whether a use now fails silently.

**Defense.** The defense gives a static argument for each family of uses:
- the field-name family has [simp] twins;
- Phi_Type.thy:82 and :102 guard a boolean, and LPR_ctrl_red_bool is [simp];
- at :4227, only SE_Has_or_Not_None makes C_R False, which forces R to be `\<circle>`. The remainder mappers are then a schematic, the literal `(\<lambda>_. unspec)`, or `?\<^sub>M False m f`, which cond_mapper_red [simp] reduces.

It rejects the shadow-comparison build as out of proportion.

**What I verified.** The simpset swap is real. SE_Has_or_Not_None is the only rule that makes C false. cond_mapper_red and LPR_ctrl_red_bool are both [simp] and [\<phi>safe_simp].

**One point the defense dismissed too quickly.** It concerns the routine failure path, where the simplifier has already turned the guard's conclusion into False.
- The judge instance stops there, by refuted_by_simp (reasoners.ML:1529-1536).
- The out-of-run instance goes on to run P-auto on a False conclusion, after the aux facts (\<phi>implication) have been inserted. That can cost up to 400 ms of thread CPU per failure.
- The old clarsimp failed fast in the same situation. So "capped versus unbounded" is not a fair comparison.
- No timing has been taken.
- The handoff's question 4, on which corpus to use and what counts as a pass, has no recorded answer.

**Ruling.** The static argument is sound as far as it goes, but it is a proof sketch, not an observation, and how much verification is enough is the author's decision. Options:
- **(a) Recommended.** The drafter documents the simpset change in section 40 and compares the Phi_Examples session times of experiments 1 and 2 against experiment 3. If experiment 3 is clearly slower, the author gets a design option: let the safe reasoner stop when guard_simp_tac leaves a False conclusion. That is prove_or_refute's step 1 1/2 without its store write, and it would make "weaker than `\<condition> P`" true by construction. It must leave p_auto_solving unchanged for the command-level callers.
- **(b)** Option (a), plus one leg with no patch that builds Bucket_Hash and PhiTest_Mem_C.
- **(c)** The shadow-comparison build. Its extra criterion, "no P-auto time within 2x of 400 ms", is rejected.

No `[\<phi>guard_simp]` declaration is added now.

### F4: the MODE_SAFE comment drops the `\<simplify>[\<safe>]` meaning (minor, and a regression this change introduced). Ruling: must discuss (user-visible text).

**What I verified.** PLPR.thy:192 now reads "as MODE_GUARD, decided cheaply, with no cache and no warning". But PLPR.thy:2161-2163 registers `\<simplify>[\<safe>]` (46 uses) as plain simplification in Phi_Safe_Simps.

**Recommendation.** Describe the mode per antecedent, and point to the `\<^descr>` block for the guard's properties, so that the "cache" and "warning" claims live in one place only. MODE_NO_INST_SAFE becomes "as MODE_SAFE in `\<condition>[\<safe>]`, prohibiting instantiation". Present F4, F5 and F6 to the author together as one text package.

### F5: "no warning" is claimed absolutely (minor). Ruling: must discuss (one yes/no question).

**What I verified.** guard_simp_tac prints "The system simplifier probably loops on:" unconditionally when its bound expires (reasoners.ML:1104-1113), and p_auto_attempt runs it first. The plan's own glossary says so (:308-309), while section 40 says "no warning", so the plan now contradicts itself. The behaviour is right: under the 2026-09-11 ruling "超时并警告之后, 保持现状", the loop warning stays, and silencing it would need a flag through the p_auto_attempt that `\<phi>guard_P_auto` shares, which would be a dirty hack.

**Ruling.** Ask the author: did "不告警" mean "no warning when the guard fails"? The recommended answer is yes. On yes, the drafter changes the internal comments to "no undecided-guard warning".

### F6: the approved `\<^descr>` paragraphs overstate three things (minor). Ruling: must discuss.

**What I verified.**
1. The undecided-guard warning goes through `warn_pretty … 1`, and the default trace level is 0, so by default it does not print.
2. reasoners.ML:1602 never writes a guard that P-auto proves with no entry, so "the decision is cached" is false on the most common path.
3. `enhance_for_guard` adds the lemma list, so the safe guard sees lemmas that earlier `\<condition>` guards in the same run added. The retired solver never saw them.

Approval of the text after a reader test does not make a false sentence true.

**Ruling.** The author picks the wording; recommended wording is in the structured items. The author also answers whether the safe guard should see the lemma list; the recommended answer is yes, documented (see proposals).

### F3: the smoke probes never run the registered configuration (downgraded from major to minor; test adequacy). Ruling: drafter decides.

**What I verified.** All three probes call `run false`, while the `\<safe>` registration uses `{can_inst=true}` and all eleven uses are `\<safe>`. On a ground CHAIN guard can_inst changes nothing, so probe 3 repeats probe 1. The probes call the ML function directly and bypass the registrations.

**Fix.**
- Drive the probes through `Phi_Reasoner.reason NONE NONE` (precedent: Guard_Export_Smoke.thy:116), so that the mode written in the proposition picks both the instance and can_inst.
- Replace the duplicate probe with a SOMETRUE pair that separates the two modes: `\<safe>` expected solved, `\<no>-\<inst>-\<safe>` expected not solved. Measure both expectations; do not assume them.
- Correct section 40's coverage sentence.
- Rerun the smoke session with a negative control.

Step (1) of the finding's fix alone, can_inst = true on a ground guard, would not be observable.

## Dismissed

- **F10 (retire MODE_NO_INST_SAFE).** The author already decided this with the same zero-use fact in front of him (handoff section 4 question 1; line 74; "赞同"). The gain is cosmetic. Removing the mode breaks the complete grid of four registrations and leaves the can_inst = false capability with no theory-level spelling. Missing coverage is F3's job, not a reason to delete a feature.
- **F11 (write the degenerate instances' shape once).** Both copies predate the change, and this change removes an inline copy rather than adding one. No ruling is relaxed and no concrete failure exists. The title misnames the pair: the glossary term is "the two degenerate instances". This is outside the change.
- **F12 (a separate mode for the cheap guard).** A mode's meaning depends on the antecedent that uses it (PLPR.thy:177-180; compare `default`). A true per-antecedent comment exists (F4). The rulings fix the spelling. The costs, including store entries lost through the whole-sequent digest, far exceed a naming gain.
- **Sub-points.** F1's "flips under load" and "within 2x of 400 ms" (the bound is thread CPU, and the criterion is new). F7's "cannot fail" and `null warnings`. F8's :1733 comment and :247 anchor.
- **The curator's dropped list** (#12, #19, #20, #23, #24, #33, #40). I concur. #23's cost aspect is taken up inside F1. #24's unbounded front rewrite is now covered by F1's simpset-change paragraph. #33 becomes a commit condition below.

## Loosening proposals for the author

1. **Retire solver_mode.** This widens the scope of the commit; it relaxes no ruling. The gain: the misleading Safe_Solver label disappears instead of being reworded. The cost: about five small edits and a compile check by restarting the REPL.
2. **The glossary term "the out-of-run instance".** Recommended: keep the term and amend its definition to say it is also the reasoner of the safe modes inside runs. Alternatively, the author renames it.
3. **Read "不用 store" as "neither reads nor writes the store".** The lemma list the context carries still reaches the safe guard. The gain: one shared p_auto_attempt, with no scrub step. The cost: completeness can vary with what the store held for earlier guards; soundness does not.
