### why

No single proposal survives on its own, and the two that claimed to answer the question directly (counting-only, injection-only) each measure one side of a two-sided criterion. What I grafted: from proposal 1, the identification of the run boundary as a value already in the code (`enhance_with_lemmas` allocates a fresh `Strhashtab` and fresh `\<r>BUDGET<i>` Frees per call, so "one consumer run" = "one call of `enhance_for_guard`", verified, four call sites and only four), the `Proof_Data` slot over `Thread_Data` for nesting and laziness, and the idea of a handoff shortlist that makes the expensive experiment small. From proposal 2, the unlimited-budget reading of the real `remaining` table as the only measurement in the budget's own unit, and the `Frequent => limit` lever that makes an effectively infinite budget behaviour-comparable. From proposal 3, the one-sided anti-starvation framing (the thread-CPU bound already makes a loop safe; the budget's job is to stop it early enough that the rest of the run can still prove the guard), the `??.unknown` name-collapse fix, and the mksimps arity check as a zero-cost item. From proposal 4, the decisive honesty that with no judge nothing about demand is observable, and the permanent exhaustion field on the `guard_decision` record. From the critiques, the uniform +2 line-anchor correction, the CPU-bound feedback that makes "verdict-neutral by construction" false, the right-censoring that makes plain quantiles unsound, and the fact that `Frequent` IS the ceiling so a multiplicative recalibration collapses two rungs. My own verification then settled four things the panel argued about without resolving. First, the hook placement: `make_parents` drops ancestor imports and the merge keeps the first survivor, Phi_Preliminary's first survivor is `Performant_Isabelle_HOL.SSymb`, so proposal 3's headline claim that a hook in PLPR_error_msg reaches the corpus is false and the plan's §1 remark is right — but simulating the real rule over the import graph shows one line in `Phi_BI/Phi_Preliminary.thy` covers every theory that actually emits guard decisions, which is far cheaper than the fourteen straggler setups proposal 3 budgeted. Second, `subgoals` is 0 or 1 in every one of the 737 logged decisions, so the "one budget across all subgoals" concern that three critiques demanded be a primary axis is empirically moot here. Third, the cold log shows R-conv firing ten times and R-nitpick 144, so R-conv gets roughly 164 consumer runs cold — it is sampleable, contrary to two critiques, but the cold leg costs about twice the stated fifteen minutes. Fourth, proved decisions run at p90 = 21 ms against a 400 ms bound, which is why capacity, not demand, is the binding question at the top of the ladder and why Stage A can settle the ceiling before any corpus run is spent. The staging is what makes this beat the alternatives on cost: Stage A may show the ladder's top two rungs are past capacity and make most of Stage B's tail analysis moot, Stage B's only irreplaceable output is the multiplier and the proved-run tail, and Stage C is the only measurement with a ground truth — and its B = 10^9 arm is a within-design control that neutralises the conditionalization confound (priority demotion plus the depth-40 gate) that would otherwise invalidate any injection comparison against a clean baseline.

### rejected

- Proposal 1's statistic C, the 'amplification factor' tier_new = tier_old x (per-run total / max-in-one-invocation): quantiles do not compose, the two factors are strongly correlated, and tier_old was fitted on a different population (the 2026-09-08 sample is a hand-written prime-checking proc, not the corpus). The per-run totals are measured directly, so read the tiers off them.
- Proposal 1's and 3's claim that the probe is verdict-neutral by construction: P-auto and R-conv run under 400 ms of THREAD CPU (reasoners.ML:1055, Thread_CPU.apply) and G.simpcut under 5 s, so every probe cycle is charged to the clock that decides verdicts. Replaced by raising the bounds tenfold on the measurement legs and measuring the censoring separately.
- Proposal 2's L1 leg injecting add.commute at nat as a rule 'not in HOL's default simpset': the report the proposal itself cites lists Groups.ab_semigroup_add_class.add.commute at 13193 total applications, so it is ambient and, being conditional once budgeted, would be outranked by sort_rrules and charged ~0 — one 15-minute leg producing zeros.
- Proposal 2's L2 leg deleting Phi_Preliminary.Big_def or Nat.One_nat_def from the guard-side simpset to obtain a per-run/per-invocation ratio for the same rule: the denominator comes from a different workload, and deleting One_nat_def changes the normal form of every numeral goal in PhiTest_Arithmetic, so the numerator is measured in a corpus that no longer behaves like the corpus.
- Proposal 2's claim that an unlimited budget removes right-censoring: it swaps budget-censoring for time-censoring, and the censoring lands exactly in the zone that discriminates 300 from 1000. Replaced by explicitly recording bounded_out and raising the bounds.
- Proposal 3's coverage analysis and its conclusion that a hook in PLPR_error_msg reaches 96 of 120 theories: derived from raw import lists rather than from make_parents (context.ML:531-533) plus first-survivor merge, and refuted by the actual surviving-parent set of Phi_Preliminary.
- Proposal 3's headline statistic, the per-run maximum over all rules: it is a maximum over an uncontrolled number of distinct rules, so it measures run size as much as per-rule demand. The matching unit is the (run, key) pair.
- Proposal 3's local = not (null (Thm.hyps_of thm)) flag as the proxy for judge-lemma shape: a judge lemma is a closed theory-level statement replayed by Thm.trivial, so it has EMPTY hyps — the flag selects the opposite population.
- Proposal 4's central thesis that one throughput scalar C decides the four tiers: the cost of a rewrite varies by orders of magnitude with the rule and the term, a growth rule's own capacity collapses as it runs, and the proposal's own limits section concedes capacity cannot be pinned within an order of magnitude while the decision rule needs a factor-of-3.3 discrimination. Capacity is kept, but only to decide the ceiling and ladder spacing, measured per arm rather than as one mean.
- Proposal 4's statistic 9 impeaching every number the plan quotes from simp_count_report.txt as possible double counting: trace_rrule has exactly two mutually exclusive call sites (raw_simplifier.ML:1065 and :1075, the two arms of one if unconditional) and increments only on SOME, so 31639 = 31639 is a two-rule loop, not double counting; the plan's named-rule figures stand (though they come from a different report block than the invocation count the plan pairs them with).
- Proposal 4's E3 sweep scored on 'the guard is still proved' for a synthetic growth rule: plan §7 states a cut-off growth rule leaves the term it grew and P-auto then usually fails whatever the budget, so the criterion measures the wrong thing. Rescored on residual term size, CPU, and scaling with the budget.
- Proposal 4's 'safe_obligation_solver produces consumer runs': grep shows enhance_for_guard occurs exactly five times in reasoners.ML (definition :1071, uses :1076, :1135, :1138, :1589) and none in safe_obligation_solver, which calls its own clarsimp_tac.
- Emitting one Event_Log record per consumer run (proposal 4): event_log.ML does one File.append per record under one global mutex, and consumer runs are far more numerous than decisions; that write is charged to the CPU bound being measured. Kept the 09-08 design of an in-ML accumulator plus one file write at report time.
- Installing the trace ops per run via Proof_Context.background_theory: it mints a theory node inside a proof and puts it on the certificate of the theorems P-auto returns — a real perturbation, not a hypothetical one.
- Budgeting the entire guard-side simpset at a uniform tier as a corpus-wide counterfactual: it needs Variable.add_fixes of thousands of names per consumer run, all charged to the 400 ms bound. Replaced by the targeted five-to-ten-rule sweep on the B5 shortlist with the B = 10^9 arm as control.
- Resetting the budget counter per simplifier invocation, which would make the tier names match their per-invocation meaning: already discarded in plan §18 because it hands a looping lemma a fresh budget each invocation, and it must stay discarded.
- Stratifying every table by subgoal count (demanded by three critiques): subgoals is 0 or 1 in all 737 logged decisions, so there is nothing to stratify on this corpus.
- Reporting run-level p99 criteria (proposals 1 and 3): with roughly 355 decisions and ~200 proved runs, a run-level p99 is the second- or third-largest observation. Quantiles are kept only at the (run, key) pair level; run-level tables report the maximum plus the full sorted tail.

### open_questions

- Does `Only_Once` mean one rewrite per lemma per consumer run, or one per derived rule per simplifier invocation? The code says the former (one budget Free per lemma, one table per run); the tier's name and whatever the judge will be told say the latter. The two readings give different numbers and only you can say which the judge is being asked to estimate.
- Should the budget stay per LEMMA (one counter shared by every rule `Raw_Simplifier.mksimps` derives from it, guard_cache.ML:145-152) or become per derived RULE? This is a design decision, not something a histogram can settle, and it changes what `Only_Once = 3` means arithmetically.
- Should `Frequent` keep being an alias for the ceiling (`budget_of_tier` returns `limit` for it, so `Int.min` collapses Normal into Frequent whenever Normal >= limit), or should it get its own number strictly below `\<phi>guard_lemma_apply_limit`?
- Approval for the temporary source edits: one `ML_file` + `setup` line in `Phi_BI/Phi_Preliminary.thy` (which re-elaborates the whole corpus), the eta-expansion plus four consumer tags plus one decision serial in `reasoners.ML`, a three-line exposure of the `remaining` table in `guard_cache.ML`, one extra field on the `guard_decision` Event_Log record, and raising `\<phi>guard_classic_prover_timeout` and the hard-coded 5 s of `simpcut_tac` tenfold for the measurement legs only.
- Is Stage C acceptable at all? It deliberately changes behaviour on the real corpus (it deletes real rules from the guard-side simpset and re-adds them budgeted), so verdict flips are expected and are the measurement. If only non-perturbing work is allowed, Stages A and B still run but the tiers can only be bounded from above, never confirmed.
- Two structural facts I verified may be phase-2 correctness problems rather than calibration inputs, and the answer changes the design rather than the numbers: a budgeted lemma is always CONDITIONAL, so (i) `sort_rrules` tries it only after every unconditional ambient rule at the same redex, and (ii) it is refused outright once `simp_depth` exceeds `simp_depth_limit` (default 40). Is it acceptable that a judge lemma competing with an ambient rule may never fire, and that no lemma fires deep in a simplification — or should the budget mechanism avoid making the rule conditional?
- Which corpus population is authoritative for the tiers: the warm run (~355 decisions, ~145 Refuted entry hits, 36 decisions at the 400 ms bound) or the cold run (~382 decisions, R-conv ~164 runs, refuted p50 893 ms)? They exercise different consumers, and the answer decides which leg's numbers set the tiers and which only cross-checks them.

## The four proposals

### Injection with an effectively infinite budget: measure each lemma's true per-consumer-run demand, then choose the cut-offs offline (angle: None)

**approach**: The measurement that step 3 needs is not "does budget B suffice" but "what is the distribution of the number of times one lemma is charged in one consumer run"; once you have that distribution, 3/30/300/1000 are evaluated offline by arithmetic, and no run has to be repeated per tier. So: inject synthetic lemmas into the real machinery, but run them at an effectively unlimited budget so that no observation is right-censored. Concretely, set the ceiling option \<phi>guard_lemma_apply_limit to 1000000 and give every injected lemma the tier Frequent; budget_of_tier then returns Int.min(1000000,1000000), so the budget simproc never returns NONE for exhaustion and the lemma behaves exactly like an ordinary conditional simp rule while the Strhashtab still records every charge (guard_cache.ML:68-71, :158-166). Each enhance_with_lemmas call allocates a fresh table, and each of the three consumers calls it exactly once per run, so "initial budget minus residual" in one table IS "charges in one consumer run" — the budget's own unit, read off directly rather than inferred from a per-invocation count times an assumed multiplier. Run one corpus leg per injected lemma (never two at once, so any behaviour change is attributable), plus a baseline leg with nothing injected for the verdict/wall-time diff. Choose the lemmas in three families: (L1) a rule that genuinely fires on corpus guards and is NOT ambient — the cleanest candidate is add.commute stated monomorphically at nat, permutative so it terminates under ordered rewriting and is not in HOL's default simpset; (L2) a rule the 2026-09-08 per-invocation report already measured, re-injected after deleting the ambient copy from the guard-side simpset only (Phi_Preliminary.Big_def at 895/invocation, Nat.One_nat_def at 31639) — this is the only way to obtain the per-run/per-invocation ratio for the SAME rule, which is exactly the number the plan is missing; (L3) a deliberate growth rule that matches real corpus terms, e.g. \<open>(a::nat) + b = (a + 0) + b\<close> (provable by simp, lhs head is +, rhs contains the lhs, so it grows without bound), run at unlimited budget on a short prefix only, to measure how many charges an unchecked loop actually achieves before the 400 ms / 5 s CPU bounds fire. The deletion in L2 is necessary, and the reason is sharper than "duplicate rules are ignored": the plan itself records (§1, line 531) that the duplicate warning does NOT fire for a budgeted rule, because the Guard_Budget premise carries a fresh Free and Thm.eq_thm_prop separates it from the ambient twin (raw_simplifier.ML:166-167). The real masking is priority — sort_rrules puts every premise-free rule ahead of every conditional one, and a budgeted rule is always conditional, so an ambient unconditional twin always wins the redex and the budgeted copy is never charged (raw_simplifier.ML:1093-1104, :1131). That is a behavioural fact worth reporting to the maintainer independently of this measurement.

**instrumentation**: Primary hook, one function: Phi_Guard_Cache.enhance_with_lemmas, guard_cache.ML:137-169. Temporary probe version does three things. (1) Line 138, read `Guard_Lemmas.get ctxt @ Probe.injected ctxt` instead of `Guard_Lemmas.get ctxt`, where `Probe.injected` returns [] unless a new temporary int config \<phi>guard_probe_leg selects a leg; the lemma thms are built once, lazily, by Goal.prove in PLPR.thy's temporary block exactly as Guard_Decision_Smoke.thy:281-285 already does, and for the L2 leg the ambient copy is removed with a `delsimps` applied to the same ctxt inside enhance_with_lemmas, so the deletion is scoped to guard-side contexts and nothing else in the corpus changes. (2) Right after `val remaining = Strhashtab.make (names ~~ map snd lemmas)` (guard_cache.ML:143), push one record {id, tag, digest, n_goals, budgets = names ~~ map snd lemmas, remaining} onto a process-global `Synchronized.var` list. ### Measure the demand, not the supply: per-consumer-run rule-application counting seeded at `enhance_for_guard`, with a one-sided (anti-starvation) acceptance rule (angle: None)

**approach**: Start from what the four numbers have to do, and notice that the two jobs are not symmetric. Containment (cut off a looping lemma) is almost entirely done by the wall/CPU clock, not by the budget: the plan's own §1 note records that an undetected unconditional loop does 100 000–500 000 rewrites in 2–3 s, i.e. ~5 µs per rewrite, so even 1000 wasted charges cost about 5 ms — nothing; and for the one shape where the budget would matter, a *growth* rule such as `g x = g (g x)`, the budget bounds the number of applications but not the term size, so 30 applications already mean a 2^30-node term and only the clock saves you. Therefore the budget buys essentially no containment that the clock does not already buy, and the only failure the numbers can actually cause is starvation: a legitimate lemma cut off before it has finished its work inside one consumer run, which shows up as a guard that used to be provable no longer being provable. That makes the measurement one-sided — we need an *upper* bound on how many times a single rewrite rule must fire inside one consumer run, and we never need to show the tiers are tight. The quantity that bounds that is directly observable without any judge: for each guard-side consumer run, take the application count of the most-applied rewrite rule in that run; the distribution of that per-run maximum, over the corpus, is exactly the demand that a judge lemma occupying the most-demanded role would face. Two corollaries decide the design: the per-run count is a *sum* over the many top-level simplifier invocations a run makes (P-auto's classical search simplifies at every node, through `addss`), so the 2026-09-08 per-invocation sample is a strict *lower* bound on it — and that sample already has `Phi_Preliminary.Big_def` at 895 in a single invocation, which is within 12 % of the `Frequent` tier before any multiplier is applied; and the counter is a mutable hashtable that is not backtracked, so rewrites performed in search branches the classical reasoner later discards are charged too, which is precisely why the run unit is dangerous and why the measurement must count *all* rewrites in the run, not only those on the successful path. The 2026-09-08 data cannot be re-aggregated to answer this: only the rendered text of `ai-artifacts/simp_count_report.txt` survives (13 "hot" invocations out of 13 821, top-5 rules each), the raw records are gone, and even the raw records never carried a run boundary, so no re-aggregation could recover per-run sums; the sample was also taken over three example theories with no restriction to the guard-side provers. New data is unavoidable, but it is cheap, because the run boundary already exists as a named function in the source. I therefore recommend approach (b), counting, with one refinement that makes it exact rather than statistical: seed the counting record at `enhance_for_guard`, which the code calls exactly once per consumer run, so that "one record" and "one consumer run" coincide by construction instead of being reconstructed by heuristics. I recommend *against* using injection (approach a) as the calibration instrument: a synthetic lemma's application count is a property of the lemma you invented, so it measures your own choice and not the corpus, and the masking problem (the simplifier ignoring a duplicate rewrite rule) forces you to mutilate the simpset before you can measure it. Injection keeps a role, but a surgical one: after counting has named the handful of (run, rule) pairs in the upper tail, remove exactly those rules from the guard simpset, re-inject them as budgeted lemmas at the tier in question, and re-prove just those guards — that converts a *suspected* starvation into an observed one, and it is the only way to confirm that a count above a tier would really have broken the proof. Two further measurements need no corpus run at all and should be done first because either could move `Only_Once` on its own: the `mksimps` fan-out (all rewrite rules derived from one lemma share a single budget counter, `guard_cache.ML:145-152`, so a lemma that splits into five rules at `Only_Once` gets three charges for five rules), and a microbenchmark of the cost of N wasted charges for a looping and for a growing rule, which is what fixes the containment side analytically.

**limits**: This measurement observes what rules that are ALREADY in the guard-side simpsets did; it cannot observe what a judge lemma will do, because no judge exists and no lemma exists. It cannot validate the judge's ability to pick the right tier, which is the dominant error term once phase 2 exists. ### Per-consumer-run rewrite counting: hook the run boundary at `enhance_for_guard`, the rule at `trace_rrule`, and report both units from one sample (angle: None)

**approach**: The decisive fact is that the budget's unit already exists as a value in the code: every call of `Phi_Guard_Cache.enhance_with_lemmas` (guard_cache.ML:137-169) builds a fresh `Strhashtab` of remaining budgets and a fresh set of `\<r>BUDGET<i>` Frees, so "one consumer run" is exactly "one call of `enhance_for_guard`" (reasoners.ML:1069), and there are exactly four such sites (1074 P-auto, 1133 and 1136 the two R-conv branches, 1587 "G.simpcut"). A counting probe therefore does not have to guess run boundaries from call stacks or timings: it stamps a run identity into the context at those four sites and counts every applied rewrite rule under a context that carries one. Counting itself reuses the 2026-09-08 hook unchanged in spirit — `Simplifier.set_trace_ops` with `trace_invoke`/`trace_rrule` (Simp_Count_Base.thy:119-148) — because `trace_rrule` wraps the entire rewrite attempt including condition proving and `check_conv` (raw_simplifier.ML:1065,1075), so a `SOME` result is one applied rewrite, which is what the budget premise charges. The single structural change to the old probe is that the per-invocation record is nested inside a per-run record rather than being the top record, which costs nothing: on each rewrite the probe bumps the invocation-local count and, in the same step, updates the run's running total, its running maximum-within-one-invocation, and the number of invocations that touched the rule — so one pass yields both units (per invocation and per run) on the same sample, and no end-of-invocation hook, pending list or pruning is needed. Having both units in one sample is the whole point: the 2026-09-08 numbers came from a different population (whole proc verifications, 32748 invocations, 1112872 rewrites), and comparing across samples would be an assumption; comparing the run total against the max-within-one-invocation for the same (run, rule) pair gives the amplification factor directly, and multiplying the old tiers by its high quantiles is the recalibration. Nesting — `guess_inst` re-entering the factory from inside a guard-side simplification — is handled correctly and for free by putting the run identity in a `Proof_Data` slot rather than in a `Thread_Data` var or a global: the inner `enhance_for_guard` overwrites the slot only in the inner context, so the inner run's rewrites are attributed to the inner run and the outer context is untouched when it returns, which is precisely the budget's own semantics (the inner run has its own `remaining` table). The probe must also clear the invocation slot when a run begins, so that a nested run starting inside an already-running simplifier invocation opens a fresh invocation record instead of inheriting the outer one. A per-decision identity, stamped into `guard_ctxt` in the factory (reasoners.ML:1601) and read where the `guard_decision` record is written (reasoners.ML:1529), lets every run be joined to its verdict, its hit state and its subgoal count, which is what separates the runs that mattered (a decision that ended `Proved`, where truncating a rule could have cost a proof) from the rest. The probe does not change any simpset, tactic or bound, so it is verdict-neutral by construction, and that can be checked by diffing the `guard_decision` log of the probe run against a clean run. What counting cannot do is tell you whether a truncated rewrite was needed: it gives an upper bound on need, which confirms a tier where the observed counts fall below it and flags exactly the rule/tier pairs worth the expensive injection experiment, so it is the right first measurement and it makes the injection experiment small and targeted rather than exploratory.

- Two `Proof_Data` slots, `Run : run option` and `Inv : inv option` (the `Inv` slot is Simp_Count_Base.thy:115-122's `Current`, unchanged in role).
- `registry : run list Synchronized.var` — runs are created from many threads (the handoff notes the corpus reloads in parallel at load 5-9), so the registry alone is synchronized.
- `begin_run consumer ctxt = Run.put (SOME (new run)) (Inv.put NONE ctxt)`. Clearing `Inv` is load-bearing: a nested run started inside an outer simplification must not inherit the outer invocation record.
- `trace_invoke {depth, cterm} ctxt`: `Run.get ctxt = NONE` → return ctxt unchanged (the fast path for all non-guard simplification); otherwise, if `Inv.get ctxt = SOME _` it is a nested invocation and the record is shared (as in the old probe); if `NONE`, increment `n_inv` and `Inv.put (SOME {run, seen = Strhashtab.empty 31})`.

Maintaining the max from the running invocation-local count is what removes the need for an invocation-end hook.
- Rule key: `Thm_Name.print (#name (#rrule args))` when non-empty, else `"anon:" ^ Hasher.compact (Hasher.terms [Thm.prop_of (#thm (#rrule args))])`. This fixes a real defect of the 2026-09-08 report, where every anonymous rule collapsed into one `??.unknown` bucket with 31639 counts: rules built from the goal's own premises are created with `Thm_Name.empty` (raw_simplifier.ML:974), and they are a different population from judge lemmas. Also record a base-name aggregation (strip the `(i)` index) because the budget is per lemma while `mksimps` may produce several indexed rules from one lemma.
- `trace_simproc` stays a pass-through but counts under `"simproc:" ^ name` in a separate namespace — the budget cannot bound simproc-driven rewriting (plan §7, lines ~1168-1172), so it must be visible and excluded from the tier tables.
- `note_decision`/`decisions : (verdict, hit, n_goals, elapsed_ms) Inttab Synchronized.var`.
- `report ()` writes `ai-artifacts/guard_run_count.csv` with three record kinds: `RUN,id,consumer,decision,theory,n_inv,total_rewrites`; `RL,run_id,rule_key,total,max_in_one_invocation,n_invocations`; `DEC,decision,verdict,hit,n_goals,elapsed_ms`. **limits**: 1. Proxy population. There are no judge lemmas, so the tiers are validated against ambient simpset rules firing on the guard side. Judge lemmas will be guard-specific, usually conditional, and plausibly fire less than generic HOL rules, so tiers derived this way are conservative (too generous) rather than too tight — but they are not a measurement of the thing itself.
2. Counting over-counts need. A classical search backtracks; a rule's applications on abandoned branches are counted. So per-run counts are an upper bound on what the proof needed. That makes the confirmation one-sided: where counts sit below a tier, the tier is safe; where they exceed it, counting cannot say whether truncation would have lost the proof. Only the injection experiment (or a deliberate truncation A/B) answers that, and table I is exactly the shortlist for it.
3. No verdict counterfactual. The probe never truncates, so it cannot show that tier T changes an outcome; it can only show how far real rewriting stands from T.
4. Slight undercount of budget charges. `trace_rrule` reports SOME only when the rewrite is applied, while the budget premise is charged during condition proving and a `check_conv` rejection afterwards costs a budget unit with no rewrite (raw_simplifier.ML:1075-1085; the plan acknowledges the same case in §7). Rare, and in the safe direction for tier sizing.
5. Only exercised paths are seen. R-conv runs only where no entry says `Refuted`, so warm runs give few R-conv samples; R-nitpick is not a lemma consumer at all. The corpus is one program family (up to `PhiTest_Arithmetic`), so the numbers are that corpus's, not phi-system's in general.
6. Simproc- and congruence-driven and classical-reasoner-driven rewriting is outside the budget by design; the probe reports it separately but the tiers cannot bound it — the wall/CPU bound remains the only guard there.
7. Aggregate cost is not per-lemma cost. Budgets are per lemma, but the lemma list accumulates across a run and a block, so N lemmas at `Normal` can together spend N x 300; the tables say nothing about how many lemmas will be simultaneously active, which is a phase-2 property.
8. Rule identity is approximate. Indexed rules from one `mksimps` expansion are grouped by base name, and anonymous rules by a hash of their proposition; a lemma whose expansion crosses those groupings would be split or merged in the tables.
9. The ±30% thread-CPU variance the handoff records means table G's time-based conclusions need that margin before any tier or ceiling is moved on their strength.

## The critiques

### verdict: flawed (lens: None)

**findings**
- THE HEADLINE CLAIM IS FALSE: unlimited budget does not remove right-censoring, it swaps budget-censoring for time-censoring, and the censoring lands exactly in the zone that discriminates the tiers. Every consumer run is already time-bounded: P-auto is `cpu_bounded_tac (guard_classic_prover_bound ctxt0)` (reasoners.ML:1451-1452) with `\<phi>guard_classic_prover_timeout` defaulting to 400 ms of THREAD CPU (reasoners.ML:1045-1049); R-conv wraps its whole `refuted ()` in the same 400 ms bound (reasoners.ML:1140); G.simpcut is `cpu_bounded (Time.fromSeconds 5)` (reasoners.ML:1586-1594). So a run at budget 1000000 does not run to the lemma's demand, it runs until the clock stops it. The censoring is INFORMATIVE — the runs that get cut are precisely the high-demand runs — so every percentile of T2 above the first censored observation is a downward-biased lower bound, and T3's 'fraction with charges > B' is an underestimate by an unknown amount at exactly B = 300 and B = 1000. Worse, for a CONDITIONAL lemma each charge costs a premise proof, so 400 ms plausibly admits only a few hundred charges; 1000 (Frequent) and possibly 300 (Normal) may be physically unreachable in a P-auto or R-conv run, which the proposal would misread as 'the tiers are safe, nothing exceeds them'. The proposal's cost section notices the timeouts only for the L3 loop leg and treats them as 'the expected observation', never as a bias on T2/T3.
- THE CENTRAL ECONOMY — 'one leg at unlimited budget, then evaluate 3/30/300/1000 offline by arithmetic, no run repeated per tier' — rests on an invalid counterfactual. Observing c charges at unlimited budget tells you whether B binds; it tells you nothing about what the run does AFTER B binds, because clamping changes the trajectory. The plan states this itself (GUARD_CACHE_QUICK_MODE_PLAN.md §7, ~line 1180: 'A growth rule that is cut off after `budget` applications LEAVES THE TERM IT GREW; P-auto then works on that term and usually fails or runs to its timeout'). Once the budget clamps, the simplifier reaches a different normal form, the classical search `nodup_depth_tac (addss ctxt) 10` (reasoners.ML:996-1002, 1009) explores a different tree, and every later consumer run in the same decision sees different goals. The criterion the brief states is an OUTCOME criterion ('a legitimate lemma must not be cut off before it has done its job', i.e. the guard still gets proved); an outcome criterion cannot be evaluated from a run in which the budget never bound. The claimed saving of per-tier reruns is therefore the design's main defect, not its main virtue.
- T4, called 'the decisive figure', is not decisive and is not even a well-defined quantity. (a) The per-run/per-invocation ratio is not a constant of the system: it decomposes into (number of simplifier invocations in the run in which the rule fires) x (firings per such invocation), and that first factor ranges from ~1 for a rule that touches one subgoal to ~all invocations for a ubiquitous rule. Measuring it on Big_def (730607 total applications, ai-artifacts/simp_count_report.txt final table) and One_nat_def (97337 total, the 31639 invocation flagged '!!!' as pathological in the same file) samples the extreme end, and the plan's proposed use — 'every tier must be multiplied by roughly that factor' — would recalibrate all four numbers off two ambient definitional unfolders maximally unlike a judge lemma. (b) Numerator and denominator are not commensurable in at least four independent ways, three of which the proposal itself documents and then discounts: the 09-08 denominator counts only SUCCESSFUL rewrites (Simp_Count_Base.thy trace_rrule only increments when `cont ctxt` returns SOME, :137-143) while a charge is spent whenever the `Guard_Budget` premise is discharged, including a check_conv rejection; the 09-08 denominator is a max over ALL 13821 corpus invocations, guard-side and not, while the numerator is guard-side only; the L2 delsimps demotes the re-injected copy below every unconditional rule (verified: sort_rrules' `is_simple` tests for a bare `Pure.eq` prop, raw_simplifier.ML:1093-1104, and `rews` walks that order, :1131), so it fires at different redexes in a different order than the ambient copy whose count is the denominator; and deleting One_nat_def (`1 = Suc 0`) from the guard-side simpset changes the normal form of every numeral goal in PhiTest_Arithmetic, so the numerator is measured in a corpus that no longer behaves like the corpus.
- SELECTION: family L1 is very likely already dead, and the contradicting evidence is inside the file the proposal cites. The proposal picks `add.commute` at nat because it is 'not in HOL's default simpset', flagging this as unverified. But ai-artifacts/simp_count_report.txt lists `Groups.ab_semigroup_add_class.add.commute` at 13193 total applications and 330 max-in-one-invocation, in goals of exactly the PhiTest_Arithmetic guard shape (`x mod i__ = 0 ==> i__ ~= x ==> ...`, entries #485/#3838/#13251/#13320). It is ambient in the corpus's simpsets, so by the proposal's own priority-demotion finding the budgeted copy is permanently outranked and will be charged ~0. That is one 15-minute leg producing a table of zeros. (Separately unchecked and load-bearing: whether a rule carrying the Guard_Budget premise is still admitted as PERMUTATIVE by mk_rrule's ordered-rewriting path at all — if not, the injected permutative copy loops instead of terminating.)
- SELECTION, the deeper version: none of L1/L2/L3 is drawn from the population the tiers describe, and the sufficiency side of the criterion — which is where 3, 30 and 300 live — therefore gets zero degrees of freedom. A judge lemma is a domain fact about one guard, proposed because P-auto failed on that guard; its demand is small and its spread is what the four tiers are meant to span. L1's demand scales with term size (permutative sorting), L2's with corpus size (ambient unfolder), L3's with the CPU bound divided by the cost of one rewrite (deliberate loop). Many consumer runs per injected lemma look like a large sample but are not independent replications of the quantity of interest: they replicate 'this one rule on this one corpus', so the effective n for the tier question is 3 or 4, all drawn off-distribution. Injection can defend the ceiling and the machinery; it cannot confirm three of the four numbers, and the proposal's decision rule ('a tier is confirmed if the legitimate lemmas' excess fraction at their intended tier is ~0') is additionally circular, since with n=3 the 'intended tier' is assigned by the maintainer after seeing the demand.
- STATISTICS THAT DO NOT ANSWER THE QUESTION. (a) Zero-inflation: most consumer runs will not fire the injected lemma at all, so P50/P90/P99 'over consumer runs' are dominated by zeros and will read as reassuring no matter what the truth is — P99 could be 0 while the tail that matters sits in the 1% above. The tiers are about demand CONDITIONAL on the lemma being relevant to that run; the report never conditions. (b) T3's conditional mean excess E[c-B | c>B] is a statistic about rewrites the budget prevents from ever happening — nobody pays that cost, and no tier decision turns on it. What the maintainer needs at each B is: how many decisions change verdict, how much CPU is saved or lost, and whether any Proved becomes Unknown. (c) T5's regression of the guard_decision record's elapsed_ms on charge count is confounded — within one leg both are driven by goal size, so the slope is not a cost per charge — and in the censored region elapsed_ms is pinned at the bound, which drags the slope toward zero. (d) T1, the one uncontaminated and genuinely missing fact (consumer runs per decision, invocations per run), needs no injection, no behaviour change and no censoring; burying it inside the contaminated design wastes it.
- THE RUN UNIT IS NOT HOMOGENEOUS, and the proposal records the covariate that would fix this and then never uses it. ### verdict: flawed (lens: None)

**findings**
- THE CENTRAL INFERENCE FAILS: the probe measures ambient simpset rules, the tiers govern judge lemmas, and the proposal's own bridge between them is an unsupported assumption. Limit 1 claims judge lemmas "plausibly fire less than generic HOL rules, so tiers derived this way are conservative". Nothing supports that direction. A judge lemma exists precisely because it rewrites THIS guard's subgoals (plan §5 step 6), so it is at least as likely to fire more per run than an ambient rule that happens to match. The sign of the bias is unknown, therefore none of D/E/F can confirm or reject a tier — they can only describe the guard-side background.
- THE AMPLIFICATION FACTOR IS ENDOGENOUS AND CANNOT BE MEASURED WITHOUT LEMMAS. guard_cache.ML:138 (`case Guard_Lemmas.get ctxt of [] => ctxt`) makes `enhance_with_lemmas` the identity when the lemma list is empty, and plan §7 states "Phase 1 has no producer of lemmas (§9)". So every run this probe observes has no budgeted rule, no `Guard_Budget` premise and no budget simproc on the chain. But "invocations per run" (statistic B) is a property of the SEARCH, and the search is `nodup_depth_tac (addss ctxt) 10` (reasoners.ML:1005-1011) whose tree shape depends on the simpset: adding a lemma changes it, and the budget biting mid-run (the rule stops matching) changes it again. The multiplier measured on a lemma-free corpus is not a constant that carries over to the process the budget will govern.
- STATISTIC C IS NOT A VALID STATISTIC. `tier_new = tier_old x A_q` composes a quantile of per-invocation counts (from the 2026-09-08 whole-verification sample, 32748 invocations) with a quantile of `total / max_in_one_invocation` (from guard-side runs). Quantiles do not compose: p99(total) is not p99(max) x p99(ratio) unless the factors are independent, and here they are strongly and unknown-signedly correlated (a rule with a high per-invocation max is typically a normaliser that also appears in every invocation). The proposal's own statistic H admits the two factors come from different populations. The per-run total distribution is measured directly by F; C adds an error term and should be deleted, not made the headline.
- RIGHT-CENSORING IS UNHANDLED AND BIASES EVERY COVERAGE NUMBER THE SAFE-LOOKING WAY. P-auto runs under `cpu_bounded_tac (guard_classic_prover_bound ctxt0)` = 400 ms thread CPU (reasoners.ML:1045-1049, 1456), R-conv under the same bound (reasoners.ML:1142), G.simpcut under 5 s (reasoners.ML:1588-1597). A run that hits its bound has its rewrite counts truncated at the timeout, and those are exactly the heavy-rewriting runs. Including them in D's "share of (run, rule) pairs with total <= T" inflates every share, i.e. makes 3/30/300/1000 look more generous than they are. No statistic records whether a run was bounded out. The probe's own overhead makes this worse in the probe run than in the control.
- THE VERDICT-NEUTRALITY CHECK CANNOT CONCLUDE. With 400 ms CPU bounds and the +-30% thread-CPU variance recorded in the handoff, some decisions flip between any two runs of the same corpus. A single probe-vs-control diff of the guard_decision log therefore cannot separate probe perturbation from ordinary flakiness; it will either show differences that are noise, or hide real perturbation inside the noise floor. A control-vs-control run is needed first to establish the baseline flip rate, and the cost section does not budget one.
- THE COUNTING KEY IS THE WRONG UNIT FOR THE BUDGET. In `enhance_with_lemmas` (guard_cache.ML:143-152) `remaining` is keyed by the per-LEMMA fixed variable name, and `weaken` attaches that same name to every rule `Raw_Simplifier.mksimps` derives from the lemma, so one budget is shared by the whole mksimps family. The proposal counts per RULE and treats base-name aggregation as a secondary column, so a lemma expanding into k rules is measured at roughly 1/k of its real charge in D/E/F. Worse, plan §7 ("A carried lemma may come back weaker") says the export hook can leave both `L` and `C ==> L` in the list as two lemmas with two budgets — a case a rule-name key cannot represent at all.
- PER-RUN TOTALS SCALE WITH SUBGOAL COUNT, WHICH IS TREATED AS A COVARIATE RATHER THAN A PRIMARY AXIS. One G.simpcut run calls `enhance_for_guard` once and then `ALLGOALS` (reasoners.ML:1589); one R-conv run enhances once and `exists`es over all goals (reasoners.ML:1135, 1138). So a single budget is spent across n_goals subgoals, while the tier is one number. Whether 300 suffices is therefore a function of the n_goals tail, and the corpus's n_goals distribution would be silently baked into any recalibration. E cross-tabulates against n_goals only for the Only_Once case.
- THE QUANTITY THAT DECIDES THE CEILING IS NOT MEASURED. Plan §7 says a bad lemma "costs its budget of rewrites in every consumer run of every guard call downstream of the one that enabled it ... until any judge answers Provable". So the damage of Frequent = 1000 is budget x runs-downstream x cost-per-application. Statistic G looks only inside ONE run against one bound, so even if it says 1000 fits in 400 ms it has not bounded the lemma's lifetime cost. The probe already stamps runs with theory and decision and could report runs-per-block cheaply, but the proposal never asks for it.
- STATISTIC G'S RATE EXTRAPOLATION IS INVALID IN EXACTLY THE REGIME IT TARGETS. It estimates the cost of 1000 applications from the ambient guard-side rewrite rate, i.e. from non-growing rules. The failure the ceiling exists for is a growth rule, whose per-application cost rises with term size — plan §7 spells this out ("A growth rule (g x = g (g x)) that is cut off after budget applications leaves the term it grew; P-auto then works on that term and usually fails or runs to its timeout"). A linear rate underestimates by an unbounded factor, so G can certify a ceiling that is in fact decorative.
- STATISTIC E ASKS THE RIGHT QUESTION BUT ITS DECISION RULE IS UNUSABLE, AND THE STRUCTURAL REMEDY IS ALREADY CLOSED. "Only_Once must be at least the p99 of per-run totals of rules whose max-in-one-invocation is 1" would push 3 up toward the invocations-per-run figure and collapse the gap to Rarely = 30 — on evidence from ambient rules, not lemmas. The obvious alternative, resetting the counter per invocation, is already ruled out in the plan's discarded list (§18: "resetting the counter per invocation (a fresh budget per invocation would hand a looping lemma a new budget each time)"). So E's honest output is a design question for the author, not a percentile, and the proposal does not say so.
- THE DECISION IDENTIFIER AS PLACED DOES NOT KEY DECISIONS UNIQUELY. The proposal stamps it into `guard_ctxt`, bound once per `apply_tac` body at reasoners.ML:1603, but `fun solving d th` (reasoners.ML:1605) is invoked once per state the case split produces, each computing its own digest `d` and calling `prove_or_refute` — hence writing its own guard_decision record at reasoners.ML:1531-1535. Two decisions can therefore share one id, silently merging their runs and verdicts in the DEC join that statistics D and I depend on.
- CONDITIONING ON `Proved` SELECTS ON THE OUTCOME. D(iii) and I restrict to runs whose decision ended Proved, on the argument that these are where truncation could cost a proof. But the runs where rewriting was most needed and still fell short are exactly the Unknown/Refuted ones, which are excluded. This trims the upper tail of the count distribution and makes every tier look sufficient.
- THE COST COMPARISON THAT MOTIVATES "COUNTING FIRST" IS WRONG ABOUT THE ALTERNATIVE. Plan §10 step 2 already builds the injection vehicle — a test-injected `Symtab` of entries exercising "a looping lemma cut off by its budget" — and step 3 as written says "the budget measured on the corpus with test-injected lemmas at each tier". The injection harness will exist regardless, so counting is an ADDITIONAL half-day plus three corpus runs, not a saving. The duplicate-masking objection ("Ignoring duplicate rewrite rule") does not apply to the plan's own design either, since test-injected lemmas need not be statements already in the simpset.
- SMALL BUT TELLING: EVERY CITED ANCHOR IS OFF BY TWO LINES. Actual anchors are `val enhance_for_guard` reasoners.ML:1071 (not 1069), `p_auto_body` :1076 (not 1074), the two R-conv sites :1135 and :1138 (not 1133/1136), `simpcut_tac`'s enhance :1589 (not 1587), `guard_ctxt` :1603 (not 1601), `Event_Log.append guard_decision_category` :1531 (not 1529). Plan §10 step 0 demands "Re-verify every line anchor individually"; a uniform +2 drift suggests the anchors were taken from a stale reading, which weakens confidence in the rest of the "verified" list.
- THE ZERO-INVOCATION COVERAGE CHECK HAS A FALSE-NEGATIVE MODE. A run can legitimately record zero simplifier invocations (an R-conv branch whose `conv_goal` raises `Unsupported` at reasoners.ML:1122-1128 never reaches the search), so "runs with zero invocations" cannot by itself distinguish a lost trace-ops hook from an empty run. Only G.simpcut, which always calls `asm_full_simp_tac`, gives a clean positive control; the check should be stated per consumer.

Capacity = C x bound is therefore only an UPPER bound on a single lemma's charges, valid in the degenerate case where the lemma is the only rule firing. The proposal's decision rule in statistic 1 ('if capacity(P-auto) < 1000, Frequent is vacuous ... if < 300, Normal is vacuous too') uses it in the sound direction, but the rest of the write-up (title: 'one scalar ... decides 3/30/300/1000') uses it in the unsound direction. An upper bound cannot set four tiers; it can only reject tiers that are too large -- and that rejection is delivered directly and without any timing by statistic 2 (total rewrites per run), which makes C inferentially redundant.
- THE CAPACITY IS A COUNTERFACTUAL THAT ALMOST NEVER MATERIALISES -- and the proposal's own cited evidence refutes its claim that it does. I re-derived the distribution from ~/.isabelle/Isabelle2025-2/event_log/guard_decision/2026-09-11T155952-2943840.xml (355 records; 204 proved / 146 refuted / 5 unknown; refuter: R-nitpick 1, none 354). Refuted decisions: mean 144 ms, p50 62 ms, p90 414 ms, max 421 ms, and only 36 of 146 are at or above 380 ms. Proved: p50 0 ms, p90 21 ms, max 125 ms. So only ~10% of all decisions (36/355) reach the 400 ms bound; three quarters of the FAILING runs terminate by search exhaustion at a fifth of the bound. The proposal's claim that 'failing ones sit at it' is true only of the maximum. Capacity = C x 400 ms therefore overstates the median failing run by roughly 6x and the median proved run by orders of magnitude, so a tier sized from capacity is sized for a case that occurs one time in ten.
- THE PROXY POPULATION IS ANTI-SELECTED. Statistic 3's 'honest proxy for judge-lemma demand' is the keys flagged `local = not (null (Thm.hyps_of thm))`. But a judge lemma is by contract a CLOSED, theory-level statement: reasoners.ML:1325-1330 ('each must be a closed statement of type prop, certifiable by Thm.cterm_of outer_ctxt and provable by its own method text citing theory-level facts only'), and replay_lemma (reasoners.ML:1397-1408) proves `Thm.trivial (Thm.cterm_of ctxt0 stmt)` by that script. Such a theorem has EMPTY hyps. The proposed flag therefore selects assumption-derived rules and systematically EXCLUDES the shape a judge lemma actually has. The one statistic advertised as answering the question is negatively correlated with it.
- SELF-CONTRADICTION BETWEEN THE HEADLINE AND THE LIMITS SECTION. Statistic 1 decides tier vacuity by comparing capacity against 300 and 1000 -- a factor-of-3.3 discrimination. The `limits` section then states that 'this analysis cannot pin capacity to within an order of magnitude', and statistic 1 itself asks only for mean and p10/p90 of C, which for a quantity spanning classical-search nodes, conditional premise proving and pure simplification will straddle at least a decade. If the interval is a decade wide, the 30-vs-300 and 300-vs-1000 boundaries are not separable and the headline statistic decides nothing.
- STATISTIC 9 ESCALATES A MISREADING INTO AN IMPEACHMENT OF THE PLAN'S NUMBERS. The `??.unknown` collapse is real (more_thm.ML:657 `get_name_hint ... the_default ("??.unknown", 0)`, reached from Simp_Count_Base.thy `rule_name`), but the inference 'its 31639 equals Nat.One_nat_def's, which smells like double counting' is wrong: raw_simplifier.ML:1063-1085 calls `trace_rrule` once per rrule application, inside exactly one rrule's `rew`, so one rewrite can never be counted under two names. 31639 + 31639 = 63278 = the reported total for #7039: that is the signature of a two-rule loop (One_nat_def against an anonymous assumption rule), not double counting. The proposal nonetheless concludes that 'every number the plan quotes from simp_count_report.txt (including the 100-445 and 895 figures) is suspect' -- those figures are NAMED rules, unaffected by the anonymous-bucket defect, so the impeachment does not follow.
- THE CENTRAL DESIGN OBJECTION (one tier scale cannot serve three consumers whose capacities differ 12.5x) compares a work allowance with a loop detector. The 5 s on 'G.simpcut' is documented at reasoners.ML:1561-1568 as 'ten times the corpus maximum of 437 ms, so a timeout means the simplifier is looping and is warned about' -- it is a diagnostic ceiling that is never meant to be reached, and on timeout the pass yields the UNSIMPLIFIED state rather than failing. Meanwhile 'G.simpcut' is one asm_full_simp per subgoal with no classical search, so its actual per-run rewrite count is plausibly SMALLER than P-auto's, not 12.5x larger. Deriving a design defect from the ratio of the two timeouts inverts the likely ordering; the proposal's own statistic 2 would settle it, and should have been made a precondition of the objection rather than a consequence of it.
- RECALIBRATING TIERS AS FRACTIONS OF A MEASURED CAPACITY (statistic 1's suggested '3 / cap/40 / cap/8 / cap/2') would break a design invariant the code deliberately establishes. Tiers are symbolic and persisted with the entry (guard_cache.ML:18-22 `type lemma = term * proof_cache * tier`; entries written by write_entry :74 and, per plan step 4, moved to a persistent store), and the budget is derived locally at read time (budget_of_tier :68-71). That is exactly what makes a stored entry portable between machines. reasoners.ML:1040-1044 records the ruling that bounds are thread CPU 'so that machine load cannot flip a verdict'. Tiers expressed as fractions of a machine-measured throughput reintroduce the machine dependence the design removed, and can make one stored entry prove on one machine and fail on another.
- E2's INJECTION PERTURBS THE QUANTITY IT MEASURES, in a way the cost section does not list. Wrapping a rule with the budget premise changes its simplifier CLASS: `Guard_Budget n ==> lhs == rhs` is no longer `Pure.eq`-headed, so `sort_rrules`/`is_simple` (raw_simplifier.ML:1095-1104) sorts it AFTER every unconditional rule at the same redex, it takes the `cond_skel` branch rather than `uncond_skel` (raw_simplifier.ML:1069 vs :1085), and -- decisively -- it becomes subject to the gate at raw_simplifier.ML:1072-1073, `if simp_depth ctxt > simp_depth_limit then give up`, with `simp_depth_limit` defaulting to 40 (raw_simplifier.ML:440). An unbudgeted rule has no such gate. So a budgeted lemma can stop firing for a reason that is NOT budget exhaustion, and a charge count alone cannot distinguish 'ran out of budget' from 'was refused at depth'. Injecting the corpus's hottest rules (which are exactly the deep-chain arithmetic normalisers `Nat.plus_nat.add_0`, `One_nat_def`, `Num.arith_simps(62)`) puts the measurement in the worst part of that confound.
- E3 IS NOT INDEPENDENT EVIDENCE AND COVERS ONLY ONE OF THE TWO CONSTRAINTS. A synthetic growth rule `g x = g (g x)` exhausts any budget in the first milliseconds; sweeping 3/10/30/100/300/1000 and watching when the guard stops being proved therefore measures how fast the loop burns CPU -- i.e. C again, the same scalar E1 measures, obtained a second way. And it addresses only the loop side of the requirement. Neither E0, E1, E2 nor E3 addresses the other side ('a legitimate lemma must not be cut off before it has done its job'), which the `limits` section concedes. A design that measures one of two opposing constraints cannot fix four tiers between them.
- E0's ARITHMETIC ARGUMENT DOES NOT FOLLOW, AND ITS SAMPLE IS THE WRONG POPULATION. It is true that one lemma's budget is shared by all rules `mksimps` derives from it (guard_cache.ML:140-153, one Free and one `remaining` slot per lemma, folded over every derived rule). But 'a lemma yielding 4 rules cannot fire each rule even once at Only_Once = 3' is a defect only under the unstated premise that every derived rule must fire; mksimps splits conjunctions and implications, and normally one branch matches. Separately, the sample proposed ('facts of judge-lemma shape harvested from the failing guards' own premises') is not judge-lemma shaped -- the judge proposes auxiliary theory facts, not restatements of the guard's premises -- so the arity histogram would characterise guard premises, not judge lemmas.
- STATISTIC 8'S VALIDITY CHECK USES THE WRONG CLOCK AND THE WRONG OBSERVABLE. `elapsed_ms` is WALL clock: reasoners.ML:1528, `Time.toMilliseconds (#elapsed (Timing.result timer))`, from a `Timing.start ()` at :1420 -- while every bound in question is thread CPU (`cpu_bounded`, reasoners.ML:1055). Calling its dispersion 'the +-30% thread-CPU noise' conflates the two. Worse, the verdict histogram is a very coarse function of the run: 204 of 355 decisions finish at p50 = 0 ms, so a probe that slows every rewrite would have to be catastrophic before the histogram moved. A check that passes almost regardless of the perturbation cannot bound the perturbation.
- THE COST OF THE COLD RUN IS UNDERSTATED, AND THE R-CONV SPLIT HAS NO SAMPLES WARM. In the cited log the refuter column is `-` 354 times and `R-nitpick` once: R-conv ran ZERO times, because all 146 refuted decisions hit an entry already marked Refuted and short-circuited at reasoners.ML:1506-1507. So every warm statistic 'split by consumer kind' has n = 0 for R-conv. The cold run needed to populate it must establish ~146 refutations through R-conv and then R-nitpick, and R-nitpick is an external model search kept on WALL clock (reasoners.ML comment at :1150-1160 and the plan's note that it refutes 222 of 247). Budgeting 'two 15-minute corpus runs' for cold plus warm is, I assume rather than verified, a large underestimate for the cold leg.
- THE PERMANENT EXHAUSTION FIELD IS UNDER-SPECIFIED FOR THE DEFECT IT IS MEANT TO DIAGNOSE. The misattributed error is real (reasoners.ML:1478, `error "The judge is broken: P-auto cannot prove the guard with the lemmas it proposed."`, reached from `case p_auto ctxt_g' of ... | NONE =>`). But a decision-level 'did any budget reach zero, and what was the max charge' conflates the two distinct P-auto runs in one decision -- step 2's `p_auto ctxt_g` (:1497) and step 6's `p_auto ctxt_g'` (:1477) -- and, more fundamentally, exhaustion is not insufficiency: a budget hitting zero does not show the lemma needed more. Without a retry at a raised limit the field records a symptom that cannot be turned into a tier number.

### verdict: sound_with_fixes (lens: None)

**findings**
- CONSUMER INVENTORY IS INCOMPLETE — `gen_guard_condition_solver` has TWO instantiations, and the proposal only accounts for one. reasoners.ML:1629 `guard_condition_judge = gen_guard_condition_solver guard_digest prove_or_refute`, but reasoners.ML:1636-1638 `guard_condition_solver' = gen_guard_condition_solver (K (K ())) p_auto_solving`, where `p_auto_solving` (:1632-1634) calls `p_auto_body` directly. That second path is live in five phi-system call sites (Phi_System/library/additions/local_value.ML:169, Phi_System/library/system/generic_variable_access2.ML:182 and :272, Phi_System/library/system/application.ML:137, Phi_System/library/system/toplevel0.ML:387). Those calls DO create budget tables (the factory's G.simpcut pass at :1589 and p_auto_body at :1076), but never enter prove_or_refute, so they emit no guard_decision Event_Log record and carry no digest (`digest_of = K (K ())`). They will silently join the histogram, and they are not in the "~355 guard decisions". T1 ("consumer runs per decision") and T6 (per-digest diff) are both unsound as written.
- THE DIGEST TAG WOULD BE EMPTY WHERE IT MATTERS — "set a Thread_Data.var around the `solving d th` call" fails because the whole guard pipeline is lazy. reasoners.ML:1589-1597: `simpcut_tac` returns `Seq.make (fn () => cpu_bounded ... Seq.pull seq ...)`; `solving d th = simpcut_tac guard_ctxt th |> Seq.maps (...)`; the factory only forces it at :1620-1622 (`|> Seq.take 1 ... |> Seq.pull`). A `Thread_Data.setmp` wrapped around the expression `solving d th` is restored before the first pull, so the simplification runs with the var unset (or holding a stale outer value from a nesting decision). Secondary obstacle: `d` is polymorphic (`digest_of : Proof.context -> thm -> 'd`), and the non-judge instantiation supplies `()`, so no digest is readable at the simpcut site generically.
- "AN EFFECTIVELY UNLIMITED BUDGET MEANS NO OBSERVATION IS RIGHT-CENSORED" IS FALSE — lifting the budget ceiling leaves the consumer run's own CPU bound in place, and that is what will truncate the tail. reasoners.ML:1050-1056: P-auto and R-conv run under `\<phi>guard_classic_prover_timeout` = 400 ms thread CPU; :1590 G.simpcut under 5 s. A high-demand lemma makes its run slower and therefore more likely to hit the bound, so exactly the part of the distribution that decides between 300 and 1000 is the part that is cut off. Worse, a truncated run is invisible in the proposed record: `cpu_bounded_seq` (:1058-1059) turns TIMEOUT into an empty Seq, `r_conv` swallows it as `false` (:1147), and only G.simpcut warns (:1591-1596).
- TABLES ARE CREATED FOR RUNS THAT NEVER EXECUTE, POLLUTING THE ZERO BIN — in `simpcut_tac` (reasoners.ML:1589) the expression `Simplifier.asm_full_simp_tac (enhance_for_guard ctxt)` is evaluated eagerly (SML strictness) when `simpcut_tac` is called, i.e. the table is allocated and pushed at that moment, while the simplification only happens when the surrounding sequence is pulled — and the factory can abandon it (`Seq.take 1` at :1618 and :1620, `quick_cut`, `head_only'`, plain backtracking). Same for `p_auto_body ctxt'` at :1453-1454, which builds the enhanced context before `Seq.pull`. "budget minus residual = 0" therefore conflates "the lemma never matched" with "this consumer run never ran".
- THE TIER/CEILING MECHANISM DESCRIBED IS NOT ON THE PATH THE PRIMARY HOOK USES — `enhance_with_lemmas` consumes `Guard_Lemmas.get : Proof.context -> (thm * int) list` (guard_cache.ML:74-78, :137-139): a plain int budget, no tier. `budget_of_tier` (guard_cache.ML:68-71) is applied only in prove_or_refute's `budget_of` (reasoners.ML:1438) for entry lemmas. So `Probe.injected` must return `(thm * int)` carrying 1000000 directly; setting `\<phi>guard_lemma_apply_limit = 1000000` has no effect on injected lemmas at all (it is harmless only because no judge exists and there are no entry lemmas).

(a) The 2026-09-08 denominators come from a different workload: ai-artifacts/Simp_Count_Base.thy:1-10 imports Phi_Semantics.* plus HOL-Computational_Algebra.Primes and installs the hook at :223 (`setup Simplifier.set_trace_ops`), and the measured program is ai-artifacts/Simp_Count_Probe.thy — a hand-written prime-checking `proc`, not the corpus, and not restricted to guard-side simplification. ### verdict: flawed (lens: None)

**findings**
- THE ACCEPTANCE RULE IS VACUOUS FOR THREE OF THE FOUR NUMBERS. The plan fixes the tiers as the JUDGE'S SEMANTIC ESTIMATE, not as quantiles of anything: Docs/GUARD_CACHE_QUICK_MODE_PLAN.md ~line 608-612 says "The tier is the judge's estimate of how often the lemma will be applied in one P-auto call", quoting the author's own words ("Only_Once, 很少 (30 次以内), 一般 (300 以内), 频繁 (1000)"). There exists no ground-truth labelling of the corpus's existing simpset rules into those four semantic classes, so a marginal distribution of per-run rule-application counts cannot say whether 30 is the right boundary between "rarely" and "normal", or whether 3 is right for "once". The proposal's criterion in statistic (1) — "a tier is confirmed iff the mass it is meant to cover falls below it" — never says what mass each tier is meant to cover, and no such mass is definable from the data it collects. Only the ceiling (Frequent = 1000, read as "no lemma should ever need more than this") gets an operational test (max < 1000). As specified, the experiment discharges §10 step 3 for one number and presents a four-row histogram as if it discharged four.
- THE HEADLINE STATISTIC IS THE WRONG ORDER STATISTIC AND IS CONTAMINATED BY A NAME-COLLAPSE BUG IT INHERITS VERBATIM. (a) "per-run maximum over rules" is a maximum over N distinct rules that happened to fire in that run, and N is uncontrolled — it varies with search size — so the max measures run size at least as much as per-rule demand. The unit that matches a budget is the (run, rule) pair; the max over rules is a different estimand. (b) Worse, the bucket that wins that max is often not a rule. ai-artifacts/Simp_Count_Base.thy:120-127 (`rule_name`) falls back to `Thm.get_name_hint`, and contrib/Isabelle2025-2/src/Pure/more_thm.ML:657 defines `get_name_hint thm = try the_name_hint thm |> the_default ("??.unknown", 0)` — a NON-empty name, so the `Thm_Name.is_empty hint` guard never fires and every anonymous rewrite rule (assumptions turned into rules by asm_full_simp, locally derived rules, exported lemmas) collapses into the single Symtab key `??.unknown`. In the real 2026-09-08 data that key is #1 by max-in-one-invocation (31639) and #2 by total (130990) — see the top-20 tables at the end of ai-artifacts/simp_count_report.txt. The proposal says it lifts the counting structure "verbatim", so statistics (1), (3) and (4) would be computed against a SUM over many anonymous rules masquerading as one rule's count.
- THE PROBE COUNTS APPLICATIONS; THE BUDGET CHARGES ATTEMPTS-THAT-REACH-THE-PREMISE, AND THE PROPOSAL DECLINES TO MEASURE THE GAP THOUGH THE INSTRUMENT COULD. Verified at contrib/Isabelle2025-2/src/Pure/raw_simplifier.ML:1075-1086: for a conditional rule, `trace_rrule` wraps the WHOLE attempt including `prover ctxt' thm'` and the subsequent `check_conv` (`:1079`); the probe (Simp_Count_Base.thy:129-139) increments only when the continuation returns SOME. Every budgeted lemma rule is conditional by construction (guard_cache.ML:145-152 appends `Guard_Budget` and rotates it last), and the budget simproc charges inside that prover call — so a `check_conv` rejection costs a charge with no application, as plan §7 (~line 1195) already records. The proposal calls this "a small known discrepancy" and quantifies it nowhere, yet the fix is free: `trace_rrule` already sees the continuation's NONE/SOME and the `unconditional` flag it currently discards. Without that, every number reported is an application count being compared against a charge budget.
- THE INSTRUMENTATION FEEDS BACK INTO THE NUMBERS THROUGH THE CPU BOUNDS, BIASING THE RESULT IN THE UNSAFE DIRECTION. Every guard-side consumer run is wrapped in a THREAD-CPU bound: reasoners.ML:1055 `cpu_bounded bound f x = Thread_CPU.apply {thread_cpu = bound, wall = NONE} f x`, used at :1454 (P-auto), :1140 (R-conv) and :1591 ("G.simpcut", 5 s). contrib/Performant_Isabelle_ML/library/thread_cpu.ML:332 registers `Isabelle_Thread.self ()` — the work runs in the SAME thread — and :128 states "Only this thread's CPU counts". A `Synchronized.change` on a Symtab per rewrite is therefore charged to the very clock that terminates the run, so instrumented runs reach FEWER rewrites before the bound fires than production runs do. The measurement thus UNDER-estimates demand, which is the unsafe direction for a bound that is supposed to be an anti-starvation UPPER bound. The proposal's remark that "the run's timings are meaningless" dismisses timing as an output and misses that timing is an input to the counts.
- THE OBSERVED TAIL IS CLOCK-SHAPED, NOT DEMAND-SHAPED, AND IS REPORTED AS IF UNCENSORED. Even with zero instrumentation overhead, no run can exhibit demand above what its CPU bound permits, so every per-run count is min(demand, clock reach) — right-censored data. The conclusion the proposal wants to draw from statistic (1) ("max < 1000, therefore Frequent = 1000 is confirmed") is exactly the conclusion censoring forbids: a ceiling can look adequate purely because the clock stopped the run first. No censoring indicator is collected and plain quantiles are proposed on censored data.
- ENDOGENEITY: THE DEMAND FOR A LEMMA IS NOT EXOGENOUS TO INSTALLING THE LEMMA, AND STATISTIC (5) READS IT BACKWARDS. The judge installs lemmas exactly where P-auto currently fails; in such a run the bounded DFS (`nodup_depth_tac (addss ctxt) 10`, reasoners.ML:986 and :1008) exhausts or times out, so the per-run counts there reflect the exhaustive search that the lemma is meant to PREVENT. The proposal presents statistic (5) (failed-run quantiles) as "the honest worst case ... these, not the easy runs, set Frequent". They are an inflated upper bound on a counterfactual that will not occur once the lemma works. Inflation is the safe direction, so this is a framing error rather than a fatal one, but (5) must not be quoted as "the demand the judge's lemma will face".
- THE PROPOSAL'S OWN CONTAINMENT ARGUMENT UNDERCUTS THE MEASUREMENT IT RECOMMENDS, AND ITS ZERO-COST ITEMS ARE MIS-SEQUENCED. The approach section argues that containment is entirely the clock's job, hence only starvation matters, hence the numbers should all be biased upward. If that argument holds, the rational recalibration is "raise every tier to the ceiling and rely on the clock" — which needs no corpus run at all, and the 15-30 minute experiment buys nothing. If it does not hold — and plan §7 (~line 1180) says a cut-off growth rule "leaves the term it grew; P-auto then works on that term and usually fails or runs to its timeout", i.e. the budget determines how bad the residue is and SMALLER is strictly better there — then containment must be measured, and this design measures only starvation. The proposal cannot have it both ways. Its items (6) mksimps fan-out and (7) cost-of-a-wasted-charge are listed as optional extras; item (7) is in fact the gate that decides whether the corpus run is worth running at all.
- THE BUDGET IS PER LEMMA, NOT PER RULE — THAT IS A DESIGN QUESTION THE PROPOSED STATISTIC CANNOT ANSWER. Verified guard_cache.ML:145-152: `weaken` maps one `\<r>BUDGETi` free over `Raw_Simplifier.mksimps ctxt1 th` and `rules = maps weaken (names ~~ lemmas)`, so all rewrite rules derived from one lemma share ONE counter. `Only_Once = 3` therefore means three rewrites TOTAL across all derived rules, not three per rule. Item (6) proposes to measure `mksimps` fan-out over existing phi-system and HOL lemmas — the wrong population (judge lemmas do not exist and need not resemble library lemmas), and, more importantly, the wrong kind of question: whether the counter should be per rule or per lemma is settled by deciding, not by sampling. Answering it with a histogram lets a design defect be laundered as a calibration constant.
- THE SET OF "GUARD-SIDE CONSUMER RUNS" DEFINED BY `enhance_for_guard` OMITS AT LEAST ONE GUARD-SIDE PROVER, AND NON-GUARD WORK WILL BE SILENTLY MIXED IN. reasoners.ML:1608 runs `cpu_bounded_tac (Time.fromMilliseconds 250) (auto_search_tac guard_ctxt) th` — the is_F shortcut — on `guard_ctxt` DIRECTLY, with no `enhance_for_guard`; the obligation instance's `fastforce` is likewise `Guard_Simp_SS`-enhanced only (plan §1). Runs of those provers would create ordinary per-invocation records with no `kind` tag and, unless a default `kind = "other"` is added and filtered, would be folded into the same `Simp_Count` summary (`snapshot ()` folds ALL pending records, Simp_Count_Base.thy:152-153). The proposal never says how non-guard records are excluded.
- NESTED NON-LEMMA WORK IS CHARGED TO THE RUN RECORD, AND THE `simp_depth_limit` CEILING ON CONDITIONAL RULES IS IGNORED. Because `trace_invoke` shares the enclosing record whenever the slot is non-empty (Simp_Count_Base.thy:113-116), every rewrite performed by anything a guard-side run calls into — a simproc re-entering PLPR, the `Guess_Instantiate` looper/solver installed at reasoners.ML:1604-1606 — is credited to that run. Those are real rewrites in the run's window but they are not rewrites a judge lemma could have made. Separately, raw_simplifier.ML:1071-1073 gives up on a CONDITIONAL rule once `simp_depth ctxt > simp_depth_limit` — so a budgeted lemma is structurally unable to fire in the deep region where much of the counted mass lives, while the unconditional library rules the probe counts fire there freely. Both effects inflate the measured "demand" relative to what a budgeted lemma could ever consume, and neither is separable from the data as collected (the probe records depth only once per record, Simp_Count_Base.thy:31-38).
- THE 2026-09-08 BASELINE THE PROPOSAL LEANS ON IS TWO DIFFERENT RUNS CONFLATED, AND THE ARITHMETIC IT QUOTES IS WRONG. ai-artifacts/simp_count_report.txt contains TWO appended report blocks (headers at line 1 and line 122, because `report_ctxt` uses `File.append`). Block 1: 13821 invocations, 155004 rewrites, 13 hot. Block 2 (lines 123-129): 32748 invocations, 1112872 rewrites, max-histogram `<10: 30476  10-99: 696  100-999: 1571  >=1000: 5`. The proposal cites block 1's invocation and hot counts together with block 2's top-20 (Big_def 895, add_0 445, One_nat_def 31639) as a single sample, and asserts "only 13 hot invocations retained" — in block 2 up to 500 of 1576 qualifying invocations are retained (`hot_limit = 500`, Simp_Count_Base.thy:57). The load-bearing conclusion (the file cannot be re-aggregated to the run unit, because no run boundary was ever recorded) survives; the supporting numbers must not be copied into the plan as fact.
- THE PROPOSAL DEMOTES THE ONLY DESIGN THAT PRODUCES A NUMBER WITH GROUND TRUTH ON THE BUDGET'S OWN UNIT. §10 step 3 asks for "the budget measured on the corpus with test-injected lemmas at each tier". Counting tells you what a rule DID; it can never tell you what a lemma NEEDED, which the proposal concedes in its limits and then papers over by treating "did N" as an upper bound for "needs N". The question "is 30 enough for a lemma the judge calls rarely" has a decidable form: take a guard the corpus currently proves, take a rule it actually used, remove that rule from the guard-side simpset (which also dissolves the duplicate-rule masking the brief flags), re-inject it as a budgeted lemma, and bisect the smallest budget at which the guard still proves. That yields a distribution of REQUIRED budgets with a proved/not-proved ground truth, on the run unit, per lemma. The proposal assigns this the role of "confirming suspects" from the counting tail; under this lens the roles must be inverted.

### verdict: sound_with_fixes (lens: None)

**findings**
- COVERAGE CLAIM IS WRONG, and wrong in the dangerous direction. The proposal computes 'first-import chains' from the `imports` list. That is not what Isabelle does. `Context.begin_thy` (contrib/Isabelle2025-2/src/Pure/context.ML:535-549) first applies `make_parents` (:531-533), which is `distinct eq_thy imports |> filter_out (fn thy => exists (fn thy' => proper_subthy (thy, thy')) thys')` -- every import that is a PROPER ANCESTOR of another import is DELETED from the parent list. Only then does `merge_data` (:447-456) fold from `data_of (hd thys)`, and `Theory_Data`'s merge (:790, `Library.foldl (fn (a,(_,b)) => Data.merge (a,b)) (#2 (hd args), tl args)`) combined with `fun merge (trace_ops, _) = trace_ops` (Pure/raw_simplifier.ML:921) keeps the first SURVIVING parent. ### verdict: flawed (lens: None)

**findings**
- HEADLINE THESIS IS A CATEGORY ERROR: throughput is not one scalar. The proposal's whole argument rests on 'the number of charges a lemma can take in one consumer run is exactly (throughput C) x (the consumer's bound)', so C is the only thing worth measuring. But the cost of one rewrite is a function of the rule and the term it acts on, and varies by orders of magnitude. A growth lemma (`g x = g (g x)`, the exact shape plan Docs/GUARD_CACHE_QUICK_MODE_PLAN.md §7 names) makes each successive rewrite act on a bigger term, so its own capacity collapses as it runs; a cheap oscillating pair on a small subterm has capacity orders of magnitude higher. Measuring mean/p10/p90 of C over the corpus's own HOL normalisation rules therefore tells you nothing about the capacity available to the one population the tiers exist to cut off. The derived conclusions ('if capacity(P-auto) < 1000, Frequent is vacuous'; 'the four tiers degenerate to {3,30,unlimited,unlimited}') do not follow from a corpus-rule mean.
- THE GROUND FOR REJECTING APPROACH (b) IS FALSE. The proposal says the `??.unknown` bucket 'smells like double counting that nobody has checked' and concludes 'every number the plan quotes from simp_count_report.txt ... is suspect'. I checked: `trace_rrule` has exactly two call sites in /home/qiyuan/Current/MLML/contrib/Isabelle2025-2/src/Pure/raw_simplifier.ML, line 1065 (unconditional branch) and line 1075 (conditional branch), and they are the two arms of one `if unconditional` — mutually exclusive, one call per attempted application. The probe (ai-artifacts/Simp_Count_Base.thy, `trace_rrule`) increments only when `cont ctxt` returns SOME. There is no double counting. The 31639 = 31639 pairing in invocation #7039 is the signature of a two-rule ping-pong loop between `Nat.One_nat_def` and one anonymous rule (total=63278 = 2x31639), i.e. exactly the pathological case the plan already calls pathological.

Line 974 is inside `mk_procrule` — the rule built from a simproc's result — a different population. Statistic 7 says the tiers 'are WRONG if the guard that was proved at budget 30 is no longer proved at budget 300 or 1000', and the approach reframes the budget as 'a throughput device whose job is to stop the loop early enough that the remaining CPU still proves the guard'. Under the plan, a looping lemma's guard is EXPECTED to fail whatever the budget; the budget bounds the damage (term growth, CPU, and the recurring cost across every downstream consumer run), it does not rescue the proof. A sweep scored on 'guard still proved' therefore measures the wrong thing.
- THE CAPACITY ARGUMENT'S EMPIRICAL CORE USES THE WRONG CLOCK AND THE WRONG QUANTITY. 'successful P-auto runs finish far below the 400 ms bound' is drawn from `elapsed_ms` in the guard_decision record, but that field is WALL clock of the whole prove_or_refute call (reasoners.ML:1529, `Time.toMilliseconds (#elapsed (Timing.result timer))`, timer started at :1420), covering lemma replay, both refuters and logging — while the bound is thread CPU (:1055, Thread_CPU.apply {thread_cpu, wall = NONE}). Worse, a proved guard stops when the proof is found; its elapsed time says nothing about the capacity it had left. Statistic 8's 'elapsed_ms p90 must stay within the +-30% thread-CPU noise' compares a wall-clock field against a CPU-noise figure.
- E2's INJECTION CHANGES RULE ORDER, SO ITS COUNTS ARE NOT THE COUNTS OF THE UNMODIFIED SYSTEM. The proposal claims E2 with an effectively infinite ceiling is 'behaviour preserving'. It is not. Every budgeted copy is CONDITIONAL (guard_cache.ML:150-152 adds the premise and rotates it last), and raw_simplifier.ML:1093-1104 `sort_rrules` partitions by `is_simple` = the prop is a bare `Pure.eq`, putting conditional rules AFTER all unconditional ones at the same net position. So converting hot unconditional rules into conditional ones reorders the whole guard-side simpset and adds a premise-proving step per application (:1075-1085). (Related: the task's stated masking mechanism is not duplicate detection — raw_simplifier.ML:166 `eq_rrule` compares `Thm.eq_thm_prop`, and a budgeted copy has a different prop, so it is never 'Ignoring duplicate rewrite rule' at :522; the masking is this ordering. `delsimps` is still required, and will also need re-doing for any rule the guard side re-derives from assumptions.)

R-nitpick invokes the external Nitpick/SAT machinery and, by the code's own comment at reasoners.ML:1054, deliberately keeps WALL clock because 'a blocked thread never reaches the limit'. 146 cold refutations through R-conv then R-nitpick is not a 15-minute run; the cost line budgets 'two 15-minute corpus runs'.

- INVERT THE EXPERIMENT ORDER: make E2 primary, not the fallback. **findings**
- LINE ANCHORS ARE UNIFORMLY +2 OFF. Every edit site named in the proposal is two lines earlier than reality in the current working tree: `enhance_for_guard` is at Phi_Logic_Programming_Reasoner/library/reasoners.ML:1071 (not 1069), `p_auto_body` 1076 (not 1074), the two `r_conv` enhances 1135/1138 (not 1133/1136), `simpcut_tac`'s enhance 1589 (not 1587), `guard_ctxt` 1603 (not 1601), `Event_Log.append` 1531 (not 1529). guard_cache.ML:68-71 and 137-169 are exact. Not a refutation — the mechanism is right — but every instruction must be re-anchored before anyone edits, and it suggests the proposal was written against a slightly older copy.
- THE FOUR TIERS ARE NOT 3/30/300/1000; `Frequent` IS THE CEILING. guard_cache.ML:68-71 reads `Int.min (limit, case tier of Only_Once => 3 | Rarely => 30 | Normal => 300 | Frequent => limit)` where `limit = Config.get ctxt guard_lemma_apply_limit` (default 1000). So (a) all four tiers are clamped by the same ceiling, and (b) `Frequent` has no number of its own — it IS the ceiling. The proposal's headline recalibration `tier_new = tier_old x A_q` would, for any A_q >= 3.34, push `Normal` to or past 1000, where `Int.min` silently collapses `Normal` and `Frequent` into the same budget and the tier system loses a level with no error. Any recalibration must be expressed as the triple (Only_Once, Rarely, Normal) plus a separate decision on `\<phi>guard_lemma_apply_limit`, under the explicit constraint Normal < limit.
- STATISTIC C (THE 'AMPLIFICATION FACTOR') IS UNSOUND AND REDUNDANT. A = total / max_in_one_invocation is not a factor you may multiply an old tier by. For any rule that fires at most once per invocation — which is exactly the population statistic E is about — max_in_one_invocation = 1 and A = total, so C is literally the per-run total under another name. Worse, tier_old was fitted to the 2026-09-08 sample's per-invocation maxima (a different population: whole `proc` verifications, 32748 invocations), so multiplying it by a ratio computed inside the NEW sample mixes two populations — precisely the cross-sample assumption the proposal claims to have eliminated. Since the same probe yields per-run totals directly (the `total` column of the RL rows), the tier should be read straight off the quantiles of per-run totals (tables D and F). C should be dropped or demoted to a descriptive column.
- VERDICT-NEUTRALITY IS NOT 'BY CONSTRUCTION' — THE PROBE'S COST IS CHARGED TO THE BOUNDS THAT DECIDE VERDICTS. P-auto and R-conv run inside `cpu_bounded (guard_classic_prover_bound ctxt)` — 400 ms of THREAD CPU (reasoners.ML:1046-1055, 1454, 1141) — and `simpcut_tac` inside `cpu_bounded (Time.fromSeconds 5)` (reasoners.ML:1591). Every per-rewrite operation the probe adds (two `Strhashtab` updates, and for anonymous rules a `Hasher.terms` digest of `Thm.prop_of`) is spent inside those budgets, so the probe can push a P-auto over 400 ms and flip a `Proved` into a `Refuted`/`Unknown`. The proposal concedes this as 'slightly tighter' but offers only the control-run diff, which DETECTS the perturbation without letting you correct for it — and a diff that shows a difference invalidates the whole run. The anonymous-rule keying makes it worse than estimated: in the 2026-09-08 sample `??.unknown` was the single largest bucket overall (simp_count_report.txt:101-102: 66160 of 1112872 rewrites, ahead of `Nat.One_nat_def` at 60533), so a cryptographic-style digest per anonymous rewrite is on the hottest path.
- THE DECISION-ID JOIN IS MANY-TO-ONE AND CAN SILENTLY MIS-ATTRIBUTE. `guard_ctxt` is constructed at reasoners.ML:1603 inside `fn (ctxt, pre, aux, _) => fn th => let val guard_ctxt = ...`, i.e. once per factory application; `solving d th` reaches `solve can_inst guard_ctxt d (ctxt0, th)` (1610) through `Seq.maps`, while the `guard_decision` Event_Log record is written once per `prove_or_refute` call (1531). So one minted id can in principle cover more than one DEC row, and the RUN-to-DEC join in the CSV would be wrong with no symptom. In practice the two `Seq.take 1` at 1617/1619 make it one — but that is an assumption the probe would be resting its whole decision-level analysis on.
- THE 'DECISION ID ABSENT/PRESENT' DISCRIMINATOR FOR P-AUTO POPULATIONS DOES NOT WORK. The proposal separates the three P-auto populations by whether a decision id is present. But the out-of-run instance goes through the SAME factory: `guard_condition_solver'` = `gen_guard_condition_solver (K (K ())) p_auto_solving` (reasoners.ML:1636-1638), so it builds `guard_ctxt` at 1603 and therefore mints a decision id and emits a 'G.simpcut' run, while never writing a DEC row (the Event_Log site is inside `prove_or_refute`, which it does not call). Only the method `\<phi>guard_P_auto` (PLPR.thy:1972-1976), which calls `p_auto_body ctxt` directly, has no id. The consumer label must therefore carry the factory INSTANCE, not be inferred from id presence. Relatedly, the in-run P-auto is not three call sites but one local `fun p_auto ctxt'` (1453-1454) reached at 1499 and — only once a judge exists — at 1473.
- SAMPLE SIZE CANNOT SUPPORT THE p99 CRITERIA AT THE RUN LEVEL. At ~355 guard decisions per corpus run, the run counts are roughly ~355 'G.simpcut' runs, ~355 P-auto runs, and a small number of R-conv runs. Criterion D(ii) ('>=95% of `Proved` runs entirely') and E's 'Only_Once must be at least the p99 of that distribution' are then estimated from single-digit numbers of runs in the tail — a p99 over ~350 observations is the 3rd or 4th largest value. Quantiles are defensible at the (run, rule) PAIR level (thousands of observations) but not at the run level; the run-level tables should report the maximum and an explicit listing of the whole tail instead of a quantile.
- R-CONV WILL BARELY BE SAMPLED EVEN ON THE COLD RUN. reasoners.ML:1498-1511: `r_conv` is reached only when `p_auto ctxt_g` returns NONE and the entry verdict is neither `Refuted` nor `Unknown`. On a cold run every entry is NONE so the `_ =>` branch is taken — but only for guards P-auto FAILED to prove, which on a healthy corpus is a small minority of the ~355 decisions. The proposal treats the cold/warm split as the thing that makes the R-conv population non-trivial; the real limiter is the P-auto failure rate, and no cold/warm arrangement fixes it. Tier numbers for R-conv are very unlikely to be supportable from this corpus at all, and the plan should say so rather than promise a per-consumer table.
- `report ()` CAN FIRE BEFORE THE CORPUS HAS FINISHED, AND THERE IS NO `reset`. phi-system proofs are forked under PIDE; a dependent theory begins as soon as the parent theory value exists, so `ai-artifacts/Guard_Run_Report.thy` importing `Phi_Test.PhiTest_Arithmetic` and evaluating `ML \<open>Guard_Run_Count.report ()\<close>` may dump a registry that is still being filled. The 2026-09-08 probe had `ML \<open>Simp_Count.reset ()\<close>` as its first command for exactly the analogous reason (ai-artifacts/Simp_Count_Probe.thy:4); this proposal has no reset, so any PIDE re-evaluation of a corpus theory double-counts into the `Synchronized.var` registry with no way to tell.
- THE 'RUNS WITH ZERO COUNTED INVOCATIONS' COVERAGE CHECK IS TOO WEAK FOR THE HAZARD IT GUARDS. The Theory_Data merge hazard the proposal identifies is real (I confirmed it, see strengths), and the consequence of missing a third merge point is silent, total data loss for a whole subtree. But a run legitimately making zero rewrites is possible, so a zero count is not a reliable signal, and the check only fires after a 15-minute run.
- THE `??.unknown` CHARACTERISATION OF THE 2026-09-08 REPORT IS HALF WRONG. The proposal says the old report 'has a single `??.unknown` bucket at 31639' and separately attributes 31639 to `Nat.One_nat_def`. Both appear in the SAME pathological invocation (simp_count_report.txt:28-29 and 79-80, each at 31639), and over the whole sample the anonymous bucket totals 66160 against `Nat.One_nat_def`'s 60533 (:101-102). Splitting anonymous rules is the right call; the number used to justify it is not the number in the file.
