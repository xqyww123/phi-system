## 4. MUST_DISCUSS — options and consequences

### MD-A (from SEM-R2-1): does the judge contract constrain the *deleted* half of a delta?

Plan §1's judge obligations and §5 step 1 constrain only `added`; `deleted` has no well-formedness clause at all, and `Pattern.equiv` is partial on ill-formed terms.

- **(a) Nothing beyond 3.2.** Apply the total `same_prop` and leave the contract silent. *Consequence:* an ill-formed deletion is silently inert and stays in the store forever; no run ever dies. Cheapest, zero design content, and sufficient to make the failure impossible.
- **(b) Symmetric contract, symmetric enforcement.** Add to §1's judge obligations "and each deleted statement is a term certifiable by `Thm.cterm_of ctxt0`"; in step 1 drop a deletion that fails to certify, with the dropped-lemma warning worded for a deletion, and let the survivors be both what is applied and what is written (self-healing, as a bad lemma already is); in step 6 a bad deletion is `error "The judge is broken: its deletion …"`, beside the existing lemma case (`reasoners.ML:1462-1464`). *Consequence:* one failure vocabulary for both halves of a delta, and the "no run-time check beyond what §5 does anyway" principle is honoured exactly, since §5 already touches these terms at `guard_cache.ML:156`. Cost: `Thm.cterm_of` is context-dependent, so a deletion dropped **here** is written back gone and lost for every later site where it would have certified — which is the same shape as the residual ruling 6 accepted for non-matching deletions, but now destructive.
- **(c) (b) for the judge only.** Error on a judge's bad deletion (step 6), leave a stored one alone (step 1) and rely on 3.2 for it. *Consequence:* keeps the loud-blame symmetry where it is useful without any destructive rewrite of a shared entry. **This is the option I would put first**, with (a) as the do-nothing.

Whatever is chosen, **3.2 should be applied now** — it is not a policy, it is a total predicate.

### MD-B (from SEM-R2-2): should the identity absorb outer `⋀`-binders?

Established fact: the replay keeps `⋀n. …` verbatim; the boundary export `gen_all`s it to `… ?n …`. So the same rule exists in the list in two shapes that `Pattern.equiv` does **not** identify, and which shape a site holds depends on whether the lemma has crossed a run or block boundary.

- **(a) Status quo + correct prose (required either way).** Say what is true in `Guard_Decision_Smoke.thy:458-459`, plan `:1121-1124` and the judge contract (`reasoners.ML:1310-1311`), and add 3.3 so the judge reads the shape off the list instead of predicting it. *Consequence:* no behaviour change; a judge that copies the statement text from `added` still silently no-ops on a lemma that has crossed a boundary, and plan §7 gains a third "a carried lemma may come back changed" case beside `C ⟹ L` and the schematic type variable.
- **(b) Peel outer binders in the comparison only.** `same_prop` compares `gen t` against `gen (Thm.prop_of th)`, where `gen` instantiates outer `Pure.all` params with fresh `Var`s — the term-level analogue of `Variable.gen_all`, applied to the two terms and discarded. Nothing is stored differently, nothing is normalised on the way in, the identity relation is still `Pattern.equiv`. *Consequence:* the two shapes of one rule become one key, a deletion written either way fires, and the documentation question stops being load-bearing. Cost, stated honestly: a lemma deliberately stated with a binder and one stated schematically can no longer coexist as two entries with two budgets — and since `enhance_with_lemmas` runs `Raw_Simplifier.mksimps` (`guard_cache.ML:200-202`), which generalises anyway, the two produce the same rewrite rules, so keeping them distinct buys two budget counters for one rule. This is a change to ruling 7's identity and is the author's alone.
